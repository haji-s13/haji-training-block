# 4-Day Lifting Routine (1 Leg Day) on a Norwegian Double-Threshold Week — v2 (16-18 upper-body sets)
**Prepared:** 2026-11-25 (v2: upper-body volume raised from ~10-15 to 16-18 sets/muscle/week; v1 kept as routine-1-leg-day-norwegian-doubles.md) | **Based on:** [report.md](report.md) (lifting evidence) and [Norwegian method vs singles](../2026-11-23-norwegian-method-vs-singles/report.md) (running structure) | **For:** Haji (83 kg, currently ~55-60 km/wk)

> Not medical advice. Build up gradually; get persistent pain checked.

## Read this first: a scale-down of Bakken's method
Bakken's model is two double-threshold days (Tue and Thu, two sessions 6-8 h apart), one harder "X" session (usually hills) and easy running the rest of the week. He ran it at ~160-180 km/week and gives 120 km as a practical minimum. Our earlier report recommended singles below ~110 km/week, and that recommendation still stands (Norwegian report, Findings 2-3, recommendation 5).

This plan therefore uses **Bakken's structure at a reduced dose**, about 65-70 km/week once built up. It is not his volume. Treat it as an experiment. If threshold sessions start to feel hard, or easy runs get harder at the same pace, go back to the singles version ([routine-1-leg-day.md](routine-1-leg-day.md)). *Confidence that this works better than singles at your volume: Low. No study has tested either method at this volume.*

## The week
| Day | AM run | PM run | Gym (PM, after running) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Easy 6-8 km | — | **Push** |
| Tue | **Threshold A:** 3 × 2 km, 60 s jog (~10 km incl. warm-up/cool-down) | **Threshold B:** 5-6 × 1 km, 60 s jog (~7 km) | **Legs**, 30-60 min after the PM run, or that evening |
| Wed | Easy 8 km | — | **Pull** |
| Thu | **Threshold A:** 4 × 6 min, 60 s jog (~10 km) | **Threshold B:** 10 × 400 m, 30 s rest (~6 km) | — |
| Fri | Rest or easy 5-6 km | — | **Upper** |
| Sat | **X session:** 10-12 × 200 m uphill, jog down (~8 km) | — | — |
| Sun | Long easy 14-16 km | — | — |

**Total running:** about 65-70 km. Threshold work is ~85 min, about 25% of running time. That is the top of the 20-25% singles guideline, which is one reason to build up gradually.

### Why the lifting sits where it does
- **Legs go on Tuesday after the second threshold session.** This follows the strength slot in the Bakken base week published on his site (Tue PM "+ strength"). It keeps all leg stress on one hard day, so Wednesday stays easy. It also leaves 48 h before Thursday's double day and 4 days before Saturday's hills. Running before lifting matches the report's concurrent-training advice (Finding 7).
- **There is no gym on Thursday or Saturday.** A double day plus legs twice a week, or legs the day before hills, is too much at this volume.
- **Upper-body days go on easy days** (Mon, Wed, Fri). Upper-body lifting does not affect running quality.

## Intensity guide (no lactate meter)
Bakken targets 2-3 mmol/L. Without a meter he says to use wider margins and cross-check heart rate with a talk test.

| Session | Pace (from your 10-mile pace ≈ 4:03/km) | Heart rate check | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold A (AM, long reps) | ~4:10-4:18 /km (half-marathon pace or a touch slower) | ≤ ~85-87% HRmax by the last rep | Could say a short sentence |
| Threshold B (PM, short reps) | ~4:00-4:05 /km (10-mile to 15 km pace) | ≤ ~87% HRmax | Controlled, never racing |
| Hills (X) | Strong but relaxed, not a sprint | n/a (too short) | Good form, full recovery on the jog down |
| Easy | 5:25-5:45 /km | ≤ ~70% HRmax | Conversational |

Bakken's PM reps are often 15-20 s/km faster than the AM. Without lactate control that would put you near 10 km pace, so this plan deliberately caps the PM at 10-mile pace. **The last rep should feel like you could do two more.**

## Gym sessions
Same principles as the main report, with upper-body volume raised to **16-18 hard sets per muscle per week**. Each upper-body muscle is trained twice a week. Upper isolation work stops at 0-1 reps in reserve (RIR), compound lifts at 1-2 RIR, and legs stay heavy at 2-3 RIR. 16-18 sets is inside the range where meta-regressions still show gains, but returns per extra set get smaller (report, Finding 1). Run the upper days as **antagonist supersets** (A1/A2: do one, rest ~60 s, do the other) to keep each session to about 70-75 minutes.

## Build-up from your current week (6 weeks)
| Weeks | Running | Lifting |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | **One** double day (Tue). Thursday is a single session (4 × 6 min). ~60-62 km | Every exercise 1 set fewer (upper body ~12-14 sets/muscle); legs at 3 RIR |
| 3-4 | Two double days, PM sessions at the low end (5 × 1 km, 8 × 400 m). ~64-66 km | Full sets (16-18 per upper-body muscle) |
| 5-6 | Full plan as written. ~65-70 km | Hold at 16-18 sets and add load or reps. Do not add more sets |
| 7 | Deload: one double day, ~50 km | Half sets, same loads |

**If recovery suffers:** cut upper-body sets back toward ~12-14 before changing the running, because this version adds about 23 sets, or roughly an hour of gym time a week. **Signs to drop back to singles:** threshold paces rising at the same heart rate, easy-run heart rate up more than ~5 bpm at the same pace, poor sleep or HRV trending down in COROS for over a week, or any niggle in the Achilles, calf or hamstring.

## Fuelling double days
Between the AM and PM sessions, eat about 1-1.2 g/kg/h of carbohydrate in the first hours (≈ 80-100 g/h at 83 kg), plus 25-40 g protein. On double days, keep daily carbohydrate at the high end (~7-8 g/kg) and protein at ~1.6-2.0 g/kg. These come from the [nutrition report](../2026-11-23-endurance-runner-nutrition/report.md). Under-fuelling double days is the quickest way to stall both running and muscle gain.

- Paces are estimated from your 10-mile pace in COROS (~4:03/km). Re-set them from a race or time trial if available.
